Large Language Models (LLMs) are a class of deep learning models trained on massive amounts of text data.
They are based on the Transformer architecture, introduced by Google in 2017 in the paper "Attention is All You Need".
LLMs can generate human-like text, answer questions, summarize documents, translate languages, and write code.
Famous LLMs include OpenAI's GPT series, Google's Gemini, Meta's LLaMA, and Anthropic's Claude.
These models are trained using a technique called next-token prediction on billions or trillions of tokens.
Fine-tuning and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) are used to align LLMs to follow instructions and behave safely.
LLMs have revolutionized natural language processing and are at the heart of many modern AI assistants.
